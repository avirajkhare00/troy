mesh · distributed synthesis · LAN

Your idle iPhones are a data-generation cluster.

A phone on a charger is a multi-TFLOPS GPU doing nothing. troy mesh puts it to work: every iPhone and Mac on your Wi-Fi runs a local teacher model and generates training data, while your Mac only coordinates — minting prompts, rejecting duplicates, validating every record. The output is a normal train.jsonl, ready for troy train.

Three commands, one shared token

The coordinator is just bookkeeping — it never loads a model, so any Mac can run it. All the GPU work happens on the workers.

  1. troy mesh serve --from ./docs --n 500

    On the Mac. Mints teacher prompts from your documents (or a --seed task description, or -f tools schemas — everything troy data synth can do), prints a join URL + token, and shows a live table of workers and throughput.

  2. TroyWorker  ·  troy mesh join

    On every device you want working. iPhones use the TroyWorker app — enter the URL and token, load a teacher from the Hub, tap Start. Other Macs join with one command. Mix freely; the mesh doesn’t care what a worker is.

  3. troy data validate  ·  troy train

    The output is byte-identical to local synthesis — the coordinator validates every record with the exact data synth code path. Spot-check it, then train. Phones make the dataset, the Mac trains on it, the phones run the result.

Built for devices that come and go

Phones lock, background, overheat, and wander off. The mesh assumes all of it — the design is pull-based and stateless on the worker side:

Workers are dumbthe coordinator renders every teacher prompt server-side; a worker just completes text and sends it back. Your source material and tool schemas never leave the Mac — phones only ever see one prompt at a time
Leases, not assignmentsevery work item has a timeout. A phone that locks mid-generation simply loses the lease; the item requeues and another worker picks it up. Kill any worker mid-run and the count still gets there
Central validationmalformed JSON, invented tool calls, and duplicates are rejected on the Mac — a flaky worker can waste its own time, but it can’t poison the dataset
Ctrl-C-saferecords append to disk as they arrive. Stop the coordinator at 300/500 and you have 300 valid records
Zero dependenciesstdlib HTTP, one bearer token, LAN-only by design. No accounts, no cloud, no telemetry — the same deal as the rest of Troy

Why data generation, not distributed training

The obvious mesh idea — shard the model, train a layer per phone — dies on contact with reality: pipeline training needs every device up simultaneously with microsecond links, and iOS suspends backgrounded apps. So the mesh runs the workload that actually fits idle phones:

  1. inference-shaped work

    Generating a training record is one prompt in, one completion out — seconds long, embarrassingly parallel, restartable, and verifiable. Exactly the shape a flaky fleet of phones can handle. Gradient-shaped work is none of those things.

  2. volume from a competent teacher

    A phone runs a 4B teacher; your Mac might run a 14B one. For style, format, and tool-calling data — most of what fine-tuning needs — a competent teacher at high volume beats a frontier teacher you can only afford to run briefly.

  3. the loop stays local

    Nothing leaves your network: not your documents, not the generated data, not the model. The same privacy story as Troy itself, now spread across every Apple device you own.

A drawer full of old iPhones is compute now.
